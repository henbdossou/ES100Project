Why I built this.

# My climate "why": rising temperatures ruin my plans

Climate change can feel like something happening to polar bears or people far away. For me it shows up in small, annoying, real ways: a game moved because of a heat advisory, an event canceled for storms, changing outfits three times because the weather won't make up its mind. When it's pouring or blazing outside, I don't even want to leave my bed — and I know my friends feel the same. Those small moments add up to a big idea: the climate is changing the plans of my generation, and our futures are the biggest plan of all.

# My superpower: programming

Programmers turn messy data into something people can use and feel. So instead of writing an essay, I wrote a program:

  • /data takes NASA's real temperature record and personalizes it — you drag to your birth year, and the code counts how many record-hot years you've lived through.
  • /run is a simulation: the same weekend plans run in different climates, with the crash odds tied to NASA's research on extreme summers. I used a game because my peers learn by doing, not by being lectured.
  • /patch turns civic actions into "commits," because changing a system takes deliberate, repeated updates — just like code.

# Who it's for

My friends and peers. They already know the planet is warming and already do the personal stuff. What I want them to see is that civic engagement — speaking up at school, contacting leaders, registering to vote — is the update that scales. The site uses the language we already speak online (errors, crashes, patch notes) and is made to be shared on TikTok.

# The data

NASA reports that Earth is warming at a rate not seen in the past 10,000 years, driven by human greenhouse gas emissions. Every stripe, line, and red square on this page comes from NASA data.
